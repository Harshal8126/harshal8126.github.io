Tips

• On most phones the speaker and main mic are both on the bottom edge. Point that edge at what you want to measure.

• Hold still. Check it against a tape measure: put a wall 50 cm, 1 m and 2 m away.

• Movement only hides everything that stays still. Wave your hand 30–80 cm away and it lights up.

• No echo? Try 14–20 kHz or 3–15 kHz. Many phone speakers are weak above 18 kHz.

• The first ~15 cm is blind because the sound going straight from speaker to mic drowns it out.

Can this find hidden rooms?

The echo view cannot see through walls or the ground. When sound in air hits a wall or soil, about 99.9% of it bounces back, so all you see is the surface. It is good for distances, room shape and movement.

Real searches for hidden rooms use ground-penetrating radar, seismic waves, or muons (as at the Great Pyramid). LiDAR sees through trees, not through the ground.

The closest a phone gets is Knock test. A knock makes the wall itself vibrate, and a cavity behind it changes the ring. Inspectors use the same "tap test" to find hollow tiles and loose plaster.
