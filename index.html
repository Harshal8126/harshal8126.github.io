How to use
  1. Put the phone flat and still on a table, speaker side free. Press Start and allow the microphone. Stay quiet for the first 1.5 s (noise measurement).
  2. Check the status line: “direct” should be at least 25–30 dB above noise. If not, raise the volume or try 14–20 kHz.
  3. Sit 40–100 cm away and breathe slowly and steadily. After about the analysis window (30 s) a moving echo should appear in the list.
  4. Don’t touch the phone while measuring. Touching it resets the history.
What it does, and every approximation
  1. Beep: Hann-windowed linear chirp, 10 ms. Range resolution c/(2·bandwidth) with c = 343 m/s.
  2. Detection: matched filter against the complex (analytic) chirp, FFT overlap-save, so each output has envelope and phase.
  3. Cycle end: mean matched-filter power in non-overlapping 10 ms blocks must stay below 2× the noise floor (+3 dB) for 5 blocks (50 ms), and not before echoes from max range have returned. Safety max 5 s. If no beep is heard within 1 s the cycle is skipped.
  4. Noise floor: median 10 ms block power during 1.5 s of silence before the first beep. After every cycle: floor ← 0.95·floor + 0.05·(power of the 50 ms quiet tail, clamped to ±50% of floor).
  5. Timing reference: direct path = strongest matched-filter peak next to the first loud block; its time is refined by parabolic interpolation of the envelope. Every echo is measured relative to it (complex ratio), which cancels latency jitter, clock drift and gain changes.
  6. Echo profile: sampled at whole-sample lags from the (fractional) direct-path time; the demodulated signal is linearly interpolated between samples. Echo peak distances use parabolic interpolation. Distance = (c·delay + speaker–mic gap)/2. The first 15 cm and the last max(5 cm, 1 resolution cell) before max range are ignored.
  7. Fine distance: phase of each echo relative to the direct path, displacement = −Δphase·λ/(4π), unwrapped over time. A static reflector at the same distance adds a constant phasor; it is removed by fitting a circle to the echo’s phasor arc (Kåsa algebraic fit, then geometric least-squares refinement) only when that centre is well defined: arc ≥ 60° around it and the points' distance from it varies less than half as much as their distance from zero. Otherwise the raw phase (centre at zero) is used, which under-reads small movements if a static reflector sits at the same distance. Plain unwrapping needs the echo to move less than λ/4 between two cycles (4.6 mm at 18.5 kHz). If more than 10% of steps exceed 90° of phase, the phase is instead unwrapped against a fitted model (line + sinusoid fitted straight to the wrapped phasors, accepted only if coherence ≥ 0.8); if that fails the echo is marked ambiguous and not reported. The graph says which method was used.
  8. Analysis rate: movement analysis reruns at most once per second of beep time.
  9. Finding candidates: for every distance, the variation of the complex echo over the window is computed; peaks above 2× the median variation are candidates (max 6). Static echoes don’t vary, so they are ignored.
  10. Period: fine-distance series linearly interpolated onto a uniform time grid (step = median cycle length), straight-line trend removed, periodogram peak between 2/window and the Nyquist limit, refined by parabolic interpolation.
  11. Size & confidence: least-squares fit of line + fundamental + 2nd harmonic on the original (non-resampled) samples. Movement = peak-to-peak of the periodic part. Confidence = share of the detrended variance explained by that fit (R²). Reported only if R² ≥ 50% and the spectral peak is ≥ 8× the median.
  12. Phone moved: compared with the average of the last 5 cycles: echo-strength profile correlation < 0.8, or direct path level change > 3 dB, or ≥ 60% of the echo peaks (at least 3) jump in phase by > 45°. Such cycles are skipped; if it lasts ≥ 3 cycles and ≥ 0.3 s, the history is cleared and the next 3 cycles are skipped.
  13. Limits: movement is only sampled once per cycle; the fastest detectable repeat is half the cycle rate (shown live). Echoes come from all directions (floor, ceiling, walls); distance is straight-line.
