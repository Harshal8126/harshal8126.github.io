How to use
  1. Put the phone flat and still on a table, speaker side free. Press Start and allow the microphone. Stay quiet for the first 1.5 s (noise measurement).
  2. Check the status line: “direct” should be at least 25–30 dB above noise. If not, raise the volume or try 14–20 kHz.
  3. Sit 40–100 cm away and breathe slowly and steadily. After about the analysis window (30 s) a moving echo should appear in the list.
  4. Don’t touch the phone while measuring. Touching it resets the history.
What it does, and every approximation
  1. Beep: Hann-windowed linear chirp, 10 ms. Range resolution c/(2·bandwidth) with c = 343 m/s.
  2. Detection: matched filter against the complex (analytic) chirp, FFT overlap-save, so each output has envelope and phase.
  3. Beep detection: the beep has arrived when the matched-filter peak in a 10 ms block exceeds 30× (15 dB) the mean noise power. Once 5 cycles with direct ≥ 20 dB have shown the playback-to-mic delay, that delay (median of the last 15) is used: triggers earlier than 20 ms before it are ignored and the direct path is searched only within ±10 ms of it, so background noise just before the beep can't be mistaken for it. After 5 cycles with no beep the learned delay is forgotten.
  4. Cycle end: mean matched-filter power in non-overlapping 10 ms blocks must stay below 2× the noise floor (+3 dB) for 5 blocks (50 ms), and not before echoes from max range have returned. Safety max 5 s. If no beep is heard within 1 s the cycle is skipped.
  5. Noise floor: median 10 ms block power during 1.5 s of silence before the first beep. After every cycle: floor ← 0.95·floor + 0.05·(power of the 50 ms quiet tail, clamped to ±50% of floor).
  6. Timing reference: direct path = strongest matched-filter peak next to the first loud block; its time is refined by parabolic interpolation of the envelope. Every echo is measured relative to it (complex ratio), which cancels latency jitter, clock drift and gain changes.
  7. Echo profile: sampled at whole-sample lags from the (fractional) direct-path time; the demodulated signal is linearly interpolated between samples. Echo peak distances use parabolic interpolation. Distance = (c·delay + speaker–mic gap)/2. The first 15 cm and the last max(5 cm, 1 resolution cell) before max range are ignored.
  8. Fine distance: phase of each echo relative to the direct path, displacement = −Δphase·λ/(4π), unwrapped over time. A static reflector at the same distance adds a constant phasor; it is removed by fitting a circle to the echo’s phasor arc (Kåsa algebraic fit, then geometric least-squares refinement) only when that centre is well defined: arc ≥ 60° around it and the points' distance from it varies less than half as much as their distance from zero. Otherwise the raw phase (centre at zero) is used, which under-reads small movements if a static reflector sits at the same distance. Plain unwrapping needs the echo to move less than λ/4 between two cycles (4.6 mm at 18.5 kHz). If more than 10% of steps exceed 90° of phase, the phase is instead unwrapped against a fitted model (line + sinusoid fitted straight to the wrapped phasors, accepted only if coherence ≥ 0.8); if that fails the echo is marked ambiguous and not reported. The graph says which method was used.
  9. Analysis rate: movement analysis reruns at most once per second of beep time.
  10. Finding candidates: for every distance, the variation of the complex echo over the window is computed; peaks above 2× the median variation are candidates (max 6). Static echoes don’t vary, so they are ignored.
  11. Period: fine-distance series linearly interpolated onto a uniform time grid (step = median cycle length), slow drift removed by subtracting a least-squares quadratic trend, periodogram peak between 3/window (at least 3 full repeats must fit in the window, so with 30 s the slowest reportable period is 10 s) and the Nyquist limit. A peak sitting on that slowest edge is treated as slow drift (drift has most power at the slowest periods) and not reported; use a longer window to see slower repeats, refined by parabolic interpolation.
  12. Size & confidence: least-squares fit of quadratic trend + fundamental + 2nd harmonic on the original (non-resampled) samples. Movement = peak-to-peak of the periodic part. Confidence = share of the detrended variance explained by that fit (R²). An echo is added to the list after 2 analyses in a row with R² ≥ 50% and spectral peak ≥ 8× the median; it stays while R² ≥ 35% and peak ≥ 5×, and is removed after 3 analyses in a row below that.
  13. Bad cycles: a cycle is ignored entirely (not analysed, not used as reference) if its direct path is weaker than 20 dB above noise or arrives more than 2.5 ms away from the learned delay – that means noise masked the beep.
  14. Phone moved: compared with the average of the last 5 good cycles: echo-strength profile correlation < 0.6, or direct path level change > 6 dB, or ≥ 70% of the echo peaks (at least 4) jump in phase by > 45°. At the first different cycle the reference is frozen and cycles are skipped for ≥ 1 s (and ≥ 3 cycles); then, if ≥ 60% of them were different, it was a move (otherwise a false alarm and measuring resumes). Small move (only the phase test fired; the echo-strength profile and direct path look the same, i.e. echoes stayed in their distance slots): measurement continues; the data before and after become separate segments, each echo’s jump is fitted as one extra unknown (a step) together with its repeating pattern, and the graph shows the joined series and the fitted jumps. Big move (in most of those cycles: the reliable echoes changed strength by > 4 dB on average, i.e. they left their distance slots, or profile correlation < 0.6, or direct path change > 6 dB; an average change > 6 dB also starts the check by itself): the history is cleared and the next 3 cycles are skipped.
  15. Limits: movement is only sampled once per cycle; the fastest detectable repeat is half the cycle rate (shown live). Echoes come from all directions (floor, ceiling, walls); distance is straight-line.
